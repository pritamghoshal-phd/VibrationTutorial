Example 6Rolling disk driven through a spring

rollingforced vibrationphase

Problem

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping. Its center O is connected to the wall by a spring $k$ and to a massless moving base B by a spring $4k$. The base moves as $x_B(t)=b\sin\omega t$. $x$ is the position of the center.

Use $m=4$ kg, $k=800$ N/m, $b=20$ mm.

Find: (a) the EOM in terms of $x$; (b) the particular solution; (c) the response amplitude at $\omega=15$ rad/s. Is it in phase or out of phase with the forcing?

Solution

Every solution follows the same five steps: coordinates, free-body diagrams, kinetics, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose a coordinate system

Let $x$ be the position of the center O, positive to the right, with both springs unstretched when $x=0$ and $x_B=0$. Let $\phi$ be the disk's rotation, positive clockwise (so rolling right means $\phi$ increases).

Step 2 · Draw the free-body diagrams
Step 3 · Kinetics (Newton–Euler equations)

Translation: $\;m\ddot x=-kx+4k(x_B-x)+F_f$

Rotation about O ($I_O=\tfrac12mR^2$): $\;\tfrac12mR^2\,\ddot\phi=-F_fR$

Friction at the bottom acting in +x turns the disk counterclockwise, hence the minus sign.

Step 4 · Kinematics (constraints)

Rolling without slipping:

$$x=R\phi\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \ddot x=R\ddot\phi$$
Step 5 · Solve

From rotation, $F_f=-\tfrac12m\ddot x$. Substitute into translation:

$$\tfrac32m\ddot x+5kx=4k\,x_B=4kb\sin\omega t,\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{10k}{3m}}$$

Particular solution: try $x_p=X\sin\omega t$:

$$X=\frac{4kb}{5k-\tfrac32m\omega^2}$$

At ω = 15 rad/s ($m=4$ kg, $k=800$ N/m, $b=0.02$ m): $5k-\tfrac32m\omega^2=4000-1350=2650$ N/m and $4kb=64$ N, so

$$X=\frac{64}{2650}=0.0242\ \text{m}=24.2\ \text{mm}$$

$X>0$, so the response is in phase with the forcing ($\omega<\omega_n=25.8$ rad/s).

Answer

(a) $\tfrac32m\ddot x+5kx=4kb\sin\omega t$   (b) $x_p=\dfrac{4kb}{5k-\tfrac32m\omega^2}\sin\omega t$

(c) $X=24.2$ mm, in phase with the forcing (since $\omega<\omega_n$).
